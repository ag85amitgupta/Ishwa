Consultant Guide

Mandate Record

How to run a search through the record — finding the mandate, screening the longlist one CV at a time, and closing the file. Written for the consultant doing the work, not for the person who built it.

Amit Gupta — AI Strategy Consultant | PiP_AGI
Before you start

What this replaces, and why

The Mandate Record replaces the earlier data entry form. It does one thing the old form could not: it captures the CVs you rejected, and why.

That sounds like extra work for no return. It isn't. Every search you run is a judgement — eighty CVs in, ten out. The ten that survive are already visible to everyone. The seventy that don't are where your actual expertise lives, and until now they left no trace. Nobody could see how you decided, review it, or learn from it.

The record captures both sides of that decision as you make it, in the same act. You attach the CV, you say why it stays or goes, you move on. Nothing is written up afterwards, because work written up afterwards never gets written up.

One rule worth knowing up front

Every CV you tag is saved the moment you tag it. There is no submit button for stage one and nothing sits in the browser waiting to be sent. If your laptop dies at CV sixty-three, CVs one to sixty-two are already in the record.

Page one

Find the mandate

Every search you run already exists somewhere in Zoho. Page one is where you connect this record to it — or, if there is nothing to connect to, where a fresh record is opened.

1
Say who you are

Pick your name from the consultant list. This is whoever is working the record right now, not who owns the mandate. If you take over a colleague's search halfway through, put your own name here.

2
Search on whatever you have

Client name, role title, Zoho ID, Ishwa mandate ID — any one is enough, and partial text works. Typing tata in client name will find Tata Motors. Give more than one field and the search narrows.

Leave every field blank and press Find, and you get the most recent mandates as a browse list. Useful when you know you worked on something but not what it was called.

3
Open the match, or open a new record

If the mandate is already on file, click it. Everything captured before — the brief, the criteria, the CVs already screened — loads back in, and you carry on where the last person stopped.

If nothing matches, you create a new record. It is given an Ishwa mandate ID automatically. You never type that ID and you never invent one.

A note on the two IDs

Two numbers travel with every mandate. The Zoho ID is Zoho's, and it comes from Zoho — you copy it across, you never make one up. The Ishwa mandate ID (ISH-YYYYMM-NNNN) is ours, and the record creates it for you. Both are stored on the same row, so a mandate can always be found from either side.

Page two

Stage one filtration

This is the eighty-to-ten screen. It is the part of the search nobody has ever recorded, and it is the reason this tool exists.

The page has two sides. Shortlisted on the left, Reject on the right. You work one CV at a time, on whichever side that CV belongs. There is no bulk upload and no upload-everything-then-tag-everything — the attaching and the reasoning happen in the same movement, because splitting them turns the reasoning into homework.

For each CV

  1. Attach the file.
  2. Type the candidate's name.
  3. Click the reasons in the word cloud — as many as apply.
  4. Add a line of free text only if the words on offer genuinely don't cover it.
  5. Press Next CV. The row saves and the panel clears for the next one.

How many reasons you have to give

SideMinimumWhy
ShortlistedOne reason. No upper limit.If a CV survives, usually one thing carried it. Click everything that applies — more is better on this side.
RejectThree reasons, or a single hard filter on its own.Three stops a reject reading as a shrug. But a hard filter is decisive by itself, and padding it with two soft reasons that weren't the real cause would make the record less honest, not more.

Hard filters are marked with a small rust square. Location mismatch with no relocation signal, compensation far out of band — the things that end a candidacy on their own. One of those is a complete answer.

If Next CV does nothing

Something is missing. A message appears telling you what — usually the file, the name, or not enough reasons selected. Fill it in and the button works.

If the words you need aren't there

Press ADD +. You give the word or phrase, and a fuller sentence explaining it. Both are saved, and the phrase is immediately available to every consultant — there is no approval step and nobody has to sign it off.

Use full words. Write notice period too long, not NP too long. Abbreviations fragment the vocabulary and the same reason ends up recorded three different ways.

Why the words all look the same size right now

The reasons are meant to grow and shrink with use — the ones your team actually reaches for become larger and rise to the top, so the list sorts itself to how Ishwa really works rather than how it was guessed at.

That sizing is calculated from real tagging. Until there is real tagging, everything is the same size. Nothing is broken; the list simply hasn't learned anything yet. It starts to differentiate once the team has screened real searches for a few weeks.

If you have no CVs to screen

Press the button at the top — it reads Skip page — no CVs screened when nothing has been tagged. Skipping writes nothing at all, which is deliberate: an empty stage one means the screen never happened, and that is different from a screen that found nobody.

Page three

The record

Everything else about the mandate lives here — the brief, the criteria, the commercials, what happened to each shortlisted candidate, and how the search closed.

The candidates carry across

Everyone you shortlisted on page two appears here automatically, with their CV already attached. You never download and re-upload a file.

For each one, say what happened:

  • Shared with client — the CV went out. Then record what came back: outcome, stage reached, and the offer detail if there was one.
  • Held back — you shortlisted them but never sent them. Say why, using the same word list.

Shortlisted and shared are not the same thing, and the record keeps them apart deliberately. Held-back candidates were never seen by the client, so counting them as rejections would be false.

Numbers you don't type

CVs shared, offers made, and the final CTC are all counted from the rows above. They cannot disagree with the file list, because they are the file list. The only figure you enter by hand is how many the client shortlisted.

Saving

Page three saves once, when you press Save. It merges — a field you left blank keeps whatever was stored before. That matters because you will come back to this record months later to close it, and a blank form must never wipe what was captured at intake.

Practical

Questions you will have

QuestionAnswer
Do I have to do this in one sitting?No. Stage one saves per CV. Page three can be saved and reopened as often as you like.
Can I edit a CV I already tagged?Not in this version. Note it and it can be corrected in the sheet.
What if I don't have the Zoho ID yet?Open a new record and carry on. The Zoho ID can be attached to the record later.
Someone else started this mandate. Can I continue it?Yes. Search for it, open it, and put your own name in the consultant field.
Where do the CVs go?A folder per mandate, named by the Ishwa mandate ID. Files are renamed to a standard pattern, and the original filename is kept alongside so a CV here can always be matched to the same CV in Zoho.
Does the client see any of this?No. This is Ishwa's internal record.
What this is building towards

Each screened CV is one example of Ishwa's judgement, written down in a form that can be read back. Enough of them, and the pattern in how this firm decides becomes visible — first as something a person can review, later as something a system can assist with.

That takes months of ordinary use, not a switch being flipped. The value of any single tagging session is small. The value of six months of them is the whole point.
