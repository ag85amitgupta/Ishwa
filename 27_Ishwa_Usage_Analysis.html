How the Sourcing Assistant is being used

24 – 31 Jul 2026
Drawn from the Sourcing_Search_Log. Covers every search saved by the delivery team in the window; internal test rows excluded. The log records searches saved — it does not record searches looked up, so recall behaviour is read from what was rebuilt rather than reused.

Who is running searches
Four of the ten consultants on the roster have not saved a search. Usage is concentrated in six people, two of whom account for half the volume.
Roster coverage
Adoption is real but partial. The gap is people, not features — every consultant who opened the tool used it more than once.
Which fields get filled
The required fields are complete on every search. The three fields that most sharply constrain the result — key skills, must-have, avoid — are used least.
Search depth by user
A 2.6× spread between the deepest and lightest user. The same tool is producing very different quality of brief depending on who is driving it.
Adoption of the 29 July release — search label and revenue / growth stage
Read as a raw fill rate, label and stage look like 25% adoption. They are not: those five rows are every search saved since the fields went live. Adoption of what was shipped is complete.
Working
They iterate rather than one-shot. 20 saved searches cover 13 distinct client-and-role briefs — consultants re-run and refine. In three consecutive searches on one mandate, the only field changed each time was avoid: someone tuning exclusions against results they were actually reading.
Not started
No one has reused a colleague's search. All 20 searches are unique — not one has ever matched another. On the single account two consultants both worked, the second rebuilt the search from scratch two days later, using a different sector and a different job title for the same role. The team is running six separate silos.
To improve
Check the log before building, and fill the three sharp fields. The consultants who discovered that avoid and must-have transform the output found it by accident; that is currently held by two people rather than the team.
